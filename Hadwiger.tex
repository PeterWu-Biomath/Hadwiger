	\section{Summary}
	In the note they construct a triangle-free graph $G$ that has many $4$-cycles, such that for the complement graph we has its independence number $\alpha(\bar G)=2$ and  connected matching number $\cm (\bar G)< m/100$ where $m$ is number of vertices. The connected matching number is defined as follows:
	
	\begin{defn}
		A matching $M$ in a graph $H$ is called a \emph{connected matching} if every two distinct edges of $M$ are joined by an edge of $H$: for all distinct $e,f\in M$, there exist $u\in V(e)$ and $v\in V(f)$ such that $uv\in E(H)$. The \emph{connected matching number} is
	\[
	\cm(H)=\max\bigl\{|M|: M\text{ is a connected matching in }H\bigr\}.
	\]
	\end{defn}
	
	The complement graph provides a counterexample to Hadwiger's conjecture, which asserts that $\chi(H)\le h(H)$, where $h(H)$ is the largest integer $t$ such that $H$ contains a $K_t$ minor. Indeed, Proposition~3.5 of the paper (proved in Appendix~\ref{app:minor-bound}) gives
	\[
	h(H)\le \frac{|V(H)|+4\cm(H)+2}{3}.
	\]
	Applying this to $H=\bar G$, and using $\alpha(\bar G)\le 2$ so that every color class has at most two vertices, we obtain, for $m$ large enough,
	\[
	h(\bar G)
	\le \frac{m+4\cm(\bar G)+2}{3}
	< \frac{26m}{75}+\frac{2}{3}
	< \frac{m}{2}
	\le \chi(\bar G).
	\]
	Thus $h(\bar G)<\chi(\bar G)$, contrary to Hadwiger's conjecture.
\section{The construction}
\subsection{Triangle-free graphs from linear algebra}
We first describe the general linear-algebraic scheme and then specify the data used in the manuscript. All vector spaces, ranks, and matrix operations are over $\mathbb F_2$.

Let $X,Y$ be finite-dimensional vector spaces. Fix $a\in X^*$ and a symmetric bilinear form $T:X\times X\to\mathbb F_2$, and put
\[
r(\lambda)=a+T(\lambda,\cdot)\in X^*.
\]
Let $V$ be the set of vertices. For each element $v\in V$, assign an injective linear map $U_v:X\to Y$ and a linear functional $u_v\in Y^*$. Then we can define the associated graph:

\begin{defn}
Two elements $i,j\in V$ is connected if there exist $\lambda_i,\lambda_j\in X$ such that
\begin{align}
U_i\lambda_i&=U_j\lambda_j,\label{eq:shared}\\
u_j\circ U_i&=r(\lambda_i),\qquad u_i\circ U_j=r(\lambda_j),\label{eq:cross}\\
a(\lambda_i)+a(\lambda_j)&=1.\label{eq:parity}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:cross} is an equality in $X^*$.
\end{defn}

\begin{lemma}
The associated graph is triangle-free.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
	
Suppose $i,j,k$ is a triangle. Write $\lambda_{ij}$ for the $\lambda_i$ at $i$ for the pair $ij$.  Evaluate (\ref{eq:cross}) on $\lambda_{ik}$:
\[
u_j(U_i \lambda_{ik})=a(\lambda_{ik})+T(\lambda_{ij},\lambda_{ik}).
\]
Then sum six equations by permuting $i,j,k$. For fixed $j$, the two terms on the left cancel because $U_i \lambda_{ik}=U_k \lambda_{ki}$. For fixed $i$, the two bilinear terms cancel by symmetry of $T$. The remaining right-hand side is
\[
\sum_{\{i,j\}}\bigl(a(\lambda_{ij})+a(\lambda_{ji})\bigr)=1+1+1=1,
\]
whereas the left-hand side is zero, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Parameters and Boolean moment spaces}
The remaining task is to choose the data so that $\bar G$ also has small connected matching number. The manuscript fixes
\[
C_0=1000,\qquad g=10^9+1,\qquad D=4C_0g,\qquad M=2^{1000}.
\]
The constants $b,j_*,J,r_0,M_0$ are chosen in the dependency order of Appendix~A of the manuscript, independently of $n$. Set
\[
h=1000r_0,\qquad N=M_0n,\qquad m=2^{C_0gN},
\]
and take $n$ sufficiently large, in particular $n\ge2r_0$ and $j_*\ge1$. The auxiliary rank and tolerance budgets used to choose these constants belong to the proof; they are not additional random variables attached to vertices.

Let
\[
I=\mathbb Z/g\mathbb Z,\quad \mathcal E=\binom I2,\quad
I_d=\{d+1,\ldots,d+(g-1)/2\}\subset I.
\]
Elements of $I$ are tags and elements of $\mathcal E$ are components. A Boolean point consists of a selector $s\in\mathbb F_2^b$ and base coordinates $z$ in $g^2+3$ blocks of $n$ bits:
\[
O_{d,t}\ (d,t\in I),\qquad S,\qquad \#,\qquad Z.
\]
Write
\[
p_* =\sum_{j=0}^{j_*}\binom bj,\qquad
B=\mathbb F_2^{p_*}\otimes\mathbb F_2^{1+(g^2+3)n}.
\]
Let $p_s$ list the evaluations of all squarefree selector monomials of degree at most $j_*$. Define the evaluation vector and its point moment by
\[
v(s,z)=p_s\otimes(1,z),\qquad w(s,z)=v(s,z)v(s,z)^T.
\]
Let $W\subseteq B\otimes B$ be the span of all point moments. For $\ell\in I$, let $W_\ell$ be the span of those point moments whose base values satisfy $O_{d,t}=0$ whenever $\ell\notin I_d$. Let $W_*$ be the span obtained by setting all $O$-blocks to zero.

The witness space is the cut-profile space
\[
X=\left\{(x_{\{\ell,\ell'\}})_{\{\ell,\ell'\}\in\mathcal E}:
x_{\{\ell,\ell'\}}=w_\ell+w_{\ell'},\quad w_\ell\in W_\ell\right\}.
\]
Thus $x$ is a tuple of matrices, and $(w_\ell)_{\ell\in I}$ is a representation of $x$. Two representations differ by a constant tuple with value in $W_*$ (Lemma~2.3 of the manuscript).

\subsection{The fixed functional and bilinear form}
Define linear testers on moments by selecting ordered matrix entries:
\begin{align*}
\eta_{d,t}(w(s,z))&=\sum_{\rho=1}^{r_0}z_{O_{d,t},2\rho-1}z_{O_{d,t},2\rho},\\
\eta_S(w(s,z))&=\sum_{\rho=1}^{r_0}z_{S,2\rho-1}z_{S,2\rho}.
\end{align*}
Put $\eta_O=\sum_{d,t}\eta_{d,t}$ and $\eta=\eta_O+\eta_S$. For a cut profile represented by $(w_\ell)$, set
\[
a_t(x)=\sum_{\ell,d}\eta_{d,t}(w_\ell),\qquad
a(x)=\sum_t a_t(x),\qquad
b_t(x)=\sum_{\ell\ne t}\eta_S(w_\ell).
\]
These functionals do not depend on the representation: the $O$-testers vanish on $W_*$, and a constant change contributes $(g-1)\eta_S(w_*)=0$ to $b_t$.

Define
\[
T_0(x,y)=a(x)a(y)+\sum_{t\in I}
\bigl(a_t(x)b_t(y)+b_t(x)a_t(y)\bigr).
\]
For each $1\le j\le J$ and ordered pair $e,f\in\mathcal E$, fix endomorphisms $L_j^{ef},R_j^{ef}$ of $B$, chosen to satisfy the uniform mixing conditions of Lemma~5.5 of the manuscript. With the entrywise pairing $\langle A,B\rangle=\operatorname{Tr}(A^TB)$, put
\begin{align*}
B_1(x,y)&=\sum_{j=1}^J\sum_{e,f\in\mathcal E}
\left\langle x_e,L_j^{ef}y_f(R_j^{ef})^T\right\rangle,\\
T_1(x,y)&=B_1(x,y)+B_1(y,x),\qquad T=T_0+T_1.
\end{align*}
Then $T$ is symmetric and $T(x,x)=a(x)$. This specifies $r(\lambda)=a+T(\lambda,\cdot)$ in the hole relation.

The prescribed self-frame matrix $E$ is also fixed. Let $E_\eta$ be the sum of the ordered tester matrices, so $\langle E_\eta,w\rangle=\eta(w)$ for $w\in W$. Choose $n\times n$ matrices $M_1,\ldots,M_b$ along with the mixers as in Lemma~5.5, and define $E_\#$ by
\[
v(s,z)^TE_\#v(s',z')=
\sum_{\rho=1}^b(s_\rho+s'_\rho)z_\#^TM_\rho z'_\#.
\]
This is bilinear on $B$ since $j_*\ge1$. Set $E=E_\eta+E_\#$. Its additional term vanishes on coincident point inputs, so
\[
\langle E,w\rangle=\eta(w)\qquad(w\in W).
\]
The $Z$-block does not occur in $E$. The mixers and the matrices $M_\rho$ are chosen once for each sufficiently large $n$, before graph sampling. Their existence with the required rank properties is supplied by Lemma~5.5; arbitrary choices need not give the connected-matching bound.

\subsection{Random frames: the raw vertex law}
For each $e\in\mathcal E$, take common ambient spaces $V_e^+=V_e^-=\mathbb F_2^N$, paired by the dot product. Write $d_B=\dim B$. A raw vertex, also called an orientation, is a tuple
\[
o=(P_{o,e},Q_{o,e},X_{o,e},Y_{o,e})_{e\in\mathcal E},
\]
where $P_{o,e},Q_{o,e}$ are $N\times d_B$ matrices and $X_{o,e},Y_{o,e}$ are $N\times h$ matrices. At each component sample uniformly among all quadruples satisfying
\begin{align*}
[P_{o,e}\;X_{o,e}]&\text{ and }[Q_{o,e}\;Y_{o,e}]\text{ have full column rank},\\
P_{o,e}^TQ_{o,e}&=E,\\
X_{o,e}^TQ_{o,e}&=0,\qquad P_{o,e}^TY_{o,e}=0.
\end{align*}
No value is prescribed for $X_{o,e}^TY_{o,e}$. The frame set is nonempty when $N\ge2(d_B+h)$, as ensured by the choice of $M_0$. Components are sampled independently; the four matrices within a component are constrained and hence need not be independent. Let $\Omega_n$ denote this finite raw vertex space and $\mu_n$ its law. The frame matrix $X_{o,e}$ is distinct from the witness space $X$.

Set
\[
V=\bigoplus_{e\in\mathcal E}V_e^+\otimes V_e^-.
\]
Identifying tensors with matrices, define
\[
U_o(x)=(P_{o,e}x_eQ_{o,e}^T)_{e\in\mathcal E},\qquad
u_o(\xi)=\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\langle Y_{o,e}X_{o,e}^T,\xi_e\rangle.
\]
The full-rank conditions make $U_o$ injective: left inverses of $P_{o,e}$ and $Q_{o,e}$ recover $x_e$ from its image. The two zero cross-Gram conditions imply $u_o\circ U_o=0$. We have therefore supplied all the data needed for the general hole relation.

\subsection{The sampled graph and the remaining theorem}
Sample
\[
o_1,\ldots,o_m\ \overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\ \mu_n,
\qquad m=2^{C_0gN}.
\]
On the positions $1,\ldots,m$, put $ij\in E(G)$ exactly when $o_i,o_j$ satisfy the hole criterion~\eqref{eq:shared}--\eqref{eq:parity}; put $ij\in E(H)$ otherwise. This completes the definition of the random graph. Its edges are determined by the sampled frames, with no further independent edge sampling.

\begin{remark}
The initial random variable is the tuple of frame matrices $o$, not a Boolean point $(s,z)$. The monomial list and moment spaces are fixed globally. Boolean points vary when constructing witnesses; sampling point parameters with the frames fixed is called a query in the manuscript.
\end{remark}

Triangle-freeness of $G$, and hence $\alpha(H)\le2$, holds for every sample. The harder assertion is that $\cm(H)<m/100$ with probability $1-\exp(-\Omega(m))$ (Proposition~3.4). To obtain it, the manuscript calls an ordered raw pair with no hole a \emph{unit}. Two units conflict if all four cross pairs are holes, so their corresponding edges cannot coexist in a connected matching.

Theorem~3.1 asserts that every law $\sigma$ on units satisfying the pointwise density bounds
\[
\sigma_1\le M\mu_n,\qquad \sigma_2\le M\mu_n,\qquad
\sigma\le2^{DN}(\mu_n\otimes\mu_n)
\]
gives a conflict between two independent $\sigma$-units with probability at least $2^{-100gN}$. The endpoints within one unit may be dependent. An entropy-based container argument transfers this statement to the sampled graph. Thus the construction above specifies the graph; the subsequent distribution theorem and container argument establish the small connected matching number needed for the counterexample.
\appendix
\section{The clique-minor bound (Proposition 3.5)}
\label{app:minor-bound}

\begin{proposition}
For every finite nonempty graph $H$ with $m$ vertices,
\[
h(H)\le \frac{m+4\cm(H)+2}{3}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(Idea) Write $c=\cm(H),\,b=h(H)$. Thus that there are $b$ pairwise disjoint, nonempty connected vertex sets, with an edge joining every pair of sets.
These sets are the branch sets of a $K_b$ minor. Let $s$ be the number of sets with one vertex and $e$ be the number of sets with two vertices.

Firstly $c>e+\frac{s-1}{2}$:
Each two-vertex branch set has an edge. And pairs of singleton branches supports an edge, the set of them is a connected matching, so we have the desired inequality. 

Then every remaining branch set has at least three vertices. Since the branch sets are disjoint,
\[
m\ge s+2e+3(b-s-e)=3b-2s-e.
\]
Combining two inequality give use the contradiction.
\end{proof}
